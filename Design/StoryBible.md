*Companion files: `Script.md`, `DevPhases.md`, `Phase1-Spec.md`, `Data/DT_Lines.csv`.*

> **SPOILERS.** Everything under "The truth" is internal. None of it is ever stated in-game.

## Logline

**Public:** Your son didn't come home from a sleepover. The man who has him wants you to play. The house is closer than you think.

**Internal:** A killer who makes parents play a game to find their children suffers a psychotic break and plays the game himself — believing he's the father, hunting the son he killed six years ago.

## Synopsis — as the player experiences it

Elias J.W. Carter, late thirties, lives in a small cottage behind a boarded-up house at the end of a rural road. He isn't well. Pills, a storm, a voice under the thunder he pretends he didn't hear. His son David is at a friend's for the night.

The phone rings. A calm, warm voice, distorted: it has David. A gunshot. A scream. An address will follow — don't call anyone. Elias stands, and the world goes to static.

He wakes on his bedroom floor. In the living room, the phone is on the carpet, a folded note sits on the side table that wasn't there before, and the front door is locked — from the outside. The note says *the key is where you keep her ring.* He knows where. A drawer. A wedding ring. A key.

Outside, rain. The text arrives: the address is the house forty feet away — the one he hasn't entered in six years. The one that was his. In the driveway sit his car and a second car he doesn't know, a child's booster seat in the back. Along the side of the house, through the trees, notes in handwriting he almost recognizes, and a megaphone bolted to the wall: *Welcome. You made it. Don't get scared, don't be afraid — after all, you're not alone.*

Inside, the house has been rearranged into a game. Locks gate every room. Newspapers cover every surface: missing families, five years of them, each child last seen at a friend's house. A boy's room upstairs with growth marks on the door frame that stop. A radio that needs batteries and the right frequency. A safe. A keypad. The voice moves inside the walls — the radio, the TV, a phone ringing in an empty room.

And Elias's mind begins to fail him. Shapes at the edge of the light. A masked figure in a doorway that isn't there when he turns. Sometimes it *is* there, and it comes, and he hides — or he doesn't, and the screen goes to static, and he wakes where he last felt safe, and something has changed. A note that wasn't there. A door that's open now.

The basement is red. The flashlight dies. Tapes click on: children crying. Then the scream from the phone — the same one. Under a sheet, a small shape. He lifts a corner. It's not him.

Deeper, a room of monitors showing every room he's walked through — and the cottage living room, the chair, the TV, from a high corner. An empty chair. A plague-doctor mask. A pill bottle with his name on it. A laminated checklist, every box ticked, in his handwriting: *relock side door. Notes — yard x6, house x9. Rewind tapes. Load call — number:* his own. A drawer of wallets and children's shoes. On a monitor, a replay: Elias, an hour ago, hiding in a closet from nothing.

A tunnel. It ends under the cottage. A foam-lined door, and a man's voice behind it, begging. Stairs up into his own kitchen. His living room. The TV is playing the phone call — and the voice on it is his. *She was going to take him, Elias. What else could we do?* The subtitle tag that has read ELIAS all game turns red.

A sound from below. The door is open. A man tied to a chair sees him and sobs with relief: *please — he's coming back — get me out.* Elias crosses to him. Then he laughs — the first time we have heard his voice — and it is the Kidnapper's. The man's face changes.

Black. Credits. Rain. A phone rings, three times.

## The truth

**Six years ago.** Elias's wife wanted to let David, nine, sleep over at a friend's. Elias — already ill, already paranoid — refused. Something would happen to him. She was going to take David and leave. That night Elias killed them both. His mind could not hold it and split: *Elias*, the father who remembers dropping David at a friend's; and *the Kidnapper*, who holds everything else.

**The ritual.** The Kidnapper takes children who are away from home at sleepovers. He calls the parent with a recorded script — his own voice, processed — and a tape of the child. He makes the parent come to the house and play it: the yard, the side door, the puzzles, the basement, the tunnel. He watches from the monitors and speaks through the walls. He appears to them in the mask. At the end, the parent finds the child dead. Then he kills the parent. He resets the house for the next one. Five years. The newspapers call them missing families.

**Tonight.** A parent was in the chamber, about to be finished. Through the mask, the parent said: *you told me you had a son.* Something broke. The Kidnapper reset the house on autopilot — every box on the checklist — locked the cottage from the outside, came back in through the tunnel, put the key in her drawer and the note on the table, loaded the call with his own number, sat down in the chair, and became the father.

**The game is the ritual.** The player walks the exact path every victim walked. Every puzzle answer is Elias's own — David's birthday, their wedding date, his handwriting. He solves them as a stranger. The apparition is himself in the mask. The blackouts are the Kidnapper, active while Elias is out. The scream on the phone was never David; there is no tape of David. It's the survivor's child.

**The loop.** After credits the phone rings again. He'll play again. He'll find the truth again. He'll forget again.

Elias's only voiced moment is the laugh in Scene 11. It is the Kidnapper's actor, unprocessed, under a red ELIAS tag.

## The property

- **The cottage** (the "outhouse") — in the backyard of the main house. Where Elias lives. Living room (chair, TV), kitchen (cellar door), bathroom, back bedrooms. First and last location of the game.
- **The main house** — the Carter family home. Boarded; front door nailed from the inside. Entered by the side door. The Kidnapper's workshop.
- **The side yard** — trees along the wall; notes; the megaphone; the positional lock on the side door. Opens onto the driveway: two cars.
- **The basement** — red rooms, the room of monitors.
- **The tunnel** — from the room of monitors to the cottage basement. Elias uses doors; the Kidnapper uses the tunnel.
- **The cottage basement** — a landing with stairs up to the kitchen, and the foam-lined chamber.

## Mechanics that carry lore

- **Panic → episode → apparition.** Nobody is in the house. The Plague Doctor is spawned only during an episode, hunts briefly, and is gone. Break line of sight, reach light, hide, or take a pill.
- **Caught = blackout, not death.** Static. Wake at the last checkpoint — *and something has changed.* The Kidnapper was active while Elias was out.
- **Pills.** Scarce. He starts with two.
- **The proof.** The monitors replay the episodes. Elias, hiding from nothing.

## Things nobody should "fix"

- The Kidnapper's first word is **"champ."** So is the birthday card's. That is not an inconsistency.
- David's room is the only room where Elias has **no line**.
- The ring stays in the drawer. He never looks at it.
- The opening cinematic's first three seconds are the **security-camera angle** the player later sees on a monitor.
- The survivor's "I have a daughter" (door pool) makes the child under the sheet a girl. Cut that one line if the tapes should stay ambiguous.
